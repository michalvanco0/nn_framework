\section{Calibration as a Measure of Trustworthiness}

A classifier can achieve high accuracy while its predicted probabilities still fail to represent uncertainty reliably. Accuracy measures the proportion of correct predictions, but does not show whether the model's probability estimates match observed outcome frequencies. This property is known as \emph{probability calibration}. A classifier is calibrated when its predicted probabilities agree with the corresponding empirical frequencies.

Let a classifier produce a probability vector $\mathbf{p}_i=(p_{i1},\ldots,p_{iK})$ for input $x_i$, where $p_{ik}$ is the predicted probability of class $k$. Let $P_k$ denote the random variable corresponding to the model's predicted probability for that class. Class-wise calibration requires that
\begin{equation}
    P(Y=k\mid P_k=p)=p, \qquad k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}.
    \label{eq:classwise-calibration}
\end{equation}
Thus, among examples for which a model assigns class $k$ a probability close to $0.8$, approximately $80\,\%$ should belong to class $k$. Calibration is a group-level property; it does not guarantee that an individual prediction is correct \cite{guo2017calibration,nixon2019measuring}.

Calibration contributes to trustworthiness because it makes a model's probability estimates meaningful for decisions based on risk. For example, a system can refer low-confidence cases for human review, while evaluation can reveal whether high-confidence predictions are still often wrong. This is relevant in applications such as medical diagnosis and autonomous systems, where confident errors can have serious consequences \cite{guo2017calibration,nixon2019measuring}. Calibration remains one part of a broader assessment: it does not guarantee that an individual prediction is correct or that a model is robust to changes in the input data.

\subsection{Calibration Evaluation}

Calibration is commonly visualized with a \emph{reliability diagram}. For each class, examples are grouped by their predicted probability for that class, and the mean probability is compared with the observed frequency of that class. A perfectly calibrated model lies on the diagonal $y=x$; points below it indicate overconfidence, while points above it indicate underconfidence. For example, a point at mean probability $0.8$ and empirical frequency $0.65$ means that the class occurred in about $65\,\%$ of the examples to which the model assigned it an $80\,\%$ probability.

Figure~\ref{fig:reliability-diagram} shows the class-wise adaptive reliability diagram generated by the calibration experiment. Each curve describes one CIFAR-10 class and can be compared with the dashed diagonal. The two insets enlarge the lower-left and upper-right regions, where many class-wise points are concentrated and small deviations from perfect calibration are difficult to see at the full scale.

The \emph{Expected Calibration Error} (ECE) is one of the most widely used summary measures of top-label calibration. It groups predictions into fixed-width confidence bins and computes a weighted average of the absolute difference between accuracy and mean confidence \cite{guo2017calibration}:
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{ECE}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{|B_m|}{n}
    \left|\operatorname{acc}(B_m)-\operatorname{conf}(B_m)\right|.
    \label{eq:ece}
\end{equation}
Here $B_m$ is the set of examples in bin $m$, $n$ is the total number of examples, $\operatorname{acc}(B_m)$ is the bin accuracy, and $\operatorname{conf}(B_m)$ is the mean top-label confidence. A lower ECE indicates a smaller average mismatch under the selected binning scheme.

Nixon et al. identify two important limitations of this ECE: it evaluates only the probability of the predicted class, and its fixed-width bins can contain very different numbers of examples. They also show that conclusions about calibration methods can depend on the metric and binning choices. Their proposed class-conditional metrics use adaptive, approximately equal-count bins. They generally recommend \emph{Adaptive Calibration Error} (ACE); for tasks with many classes, such as 100 or more, they recommend thresholded ACE (TACE), which focuses on probabilities above a threshold \cite{nixon2019measuring}.

This thesis uses ACE as its primary calibration metric. The classifier has ten CIFAR-10 classes, so ACE is a suitable choice under Nixon et al.'s recommendation; TACE is intended for settings with many classes. ACE evaluates probabilities for every class, not only the class selected as the model's prediction, and adaptive bins reduce the imbalance caused by fixed-width intervals. For $K$ classes and $R$ adaptive bins per class, it is computed as
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{ACE}=\frac{1}{KR}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{r=1}^{R}
    \left|\operatorname{acc}(B_{k,r})-\operatorname{conf}(B_{k,r})\right|,
    \label{eq:ace}
\end{equation}
where $B_{k,r}$ contains examples in the $r$-th adaptive bin for class $k$. Within that bin, accuracy is the empirical frequency of class $k$, and confidence is the mean predicted probability for class $k$. The implementation selects $R$ using Sturges' rule, $R=\lceil\log_2(n)+1\rceil$, which gives 15 bins for the 10,000-example CIFAR-10 test set. ACE remains a binned estimate and therefore depends on the number of bins and the evaluation sample. Per-class ACE values and reliability curves are reported alongside the macro-average to make class-level differences visible \cite{nixon2019measuring}.

In addition to the curve-based diagram, the experiment produces bar-based diagrams that show the bin accuracy and its calibration gap, following the presentation in Figure~\ref{fig:ece-bin-diagram}. In the top-label ECE diagram, each blue bar is the empirical accuracy of a fixed-width confidence bin. The hatched red segment shows the absolute difference between that accuracy and the bin's mean confidence; the dashed diagonal marks perfect calibration. The displayed ECE is the weighted aggregate of these gaps, as defined in Equation~\eqref{eq:ece}.

    \caption{Fixed-width top-label ECE diagram for the ResNet-18 classifier on CIFAR-10. Blue bars show bin accuracy; hatched red segments show the gap to mean confidence. The dashed line represents perfect calibration.}
    \label{fig:ece-bin-diagram}
\end{figure}

The second bar-based diagram focuses on a selected class. The value displayed for class $k$ is the class-wise component of ACE,
\begin{equation}
    \operatorname{ACE}_k=\frac{1}{R}\sum_{r=1}^{R}
    \left|\operatorname{acc}(B_{k,r})-
    \operatorname{conf}(B_{k,r})\right|,
    \label{eq:classwise-ace}
\end{equation}
which averages the gaps over that class's adaptive bins; the overall ACE in Equation~\eqref{eq:ace} then averages these class-wise values over all $K$ classes. In Figure~\ref{fig:ace-bin-diagram}, blue bars show the empirical frequency of the selected class in each bin, while hatched red segments show the gap to its mean predicted probability. The bins are ordered by increasing mean class probability, which is printed below each bin index. This diagram therefore exposes the bin-level contributions for one class, while the curve-based diagram summarizes all classes.

\subsection{Research Developments and Applications}

Guo et al. showed that increased classification accuracy does not necessarily produce better-calibrated predictions. In their experiments, network depth, width, batch normalization, and weight decay affected calibration. They also found that the post-hoc method \emph{temperature scaling} often calibrated predictions effectively. It divides logits $z_{ik}$ by a positive scalar $T$ before applying softmax \cite{guo2017calibration}:
\begin{equation}
    p_{ik}^{(T)}=\frac{\exp(z_{ik}/T)}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}\exp(z_{ij}/T)}.
    \label{eq:temperature-scaling}
\end{equation}
Because $T>0$, the predicted class does not change, but the sharpness of the probability distribution does. The parameter is fitted on a separate calibration set. The present implementation evaluates calibration but does not apply temperature scaling; fitting it would require a held-out calibration split, separate from the test set.

\section{Implementation of the Calibration Module}

The experiment in \texttt{experiments/calibration.py} loads the CIFAR-10 test split and a trained ResNet-18 checkpoint. The \texttt{evaluate} function in \texttt{evaluation/evaluator.py} switches the model to evaluation mode, computes logits without gradients, and returns softmax probabilities and ground-truth labels. ACE uses the full probability vector for each example.

The \texttt{calculate\_ace} function in \texttt{evaluation/calibration.py} processes each class probability separately. For each class, it sorts all examples by their predicted probability for that class and divides them into $R$ adaptive bins with approximately equal numbers of examples. In each bin, it compares the mean probability for the class with the empirical frequency of that class. The ACE is the unweighted average of these absolute gaps across classes and bins, as defined in Equation~\eqref{eq:ace}. The experiment selects $R$ using Sturges' rule.

The \texttt{calculate\_ece} function computes conventional top-label ECE from each example's predicted label and maximum softmax probability, using 15 fixed-width bins by default. The experiment also calculates class-wise ACE with $R$ adaptive bins. The selected class for the additional class-wise diagram defaults to cat and can be changed with the \texttt{--comparison-class} command-line option, using either a CIFAR-10 class name or its index. The fixed-width ECE bin count can be changed with \texttt{--ece-bins}. The \texttt{save\_ece\_reliability\_diagram} function draws the top-label accuracy bars, confidence gaps, and perfect-calibration diagonal. The \texttt{save\_classwise\_ace\_reliability\_diagram} function draws the selected class's adaptive-bin frequencies and gaps; it labels bins by their order and mean class probability.

The \texttt{save\_classwise\_reliability\_diagram} function plots the complete probability range and adds two inset axes for the intervals $[0,0.10]$ and $[0.90,1.00]$ on both axes. Dotted rectangles mark the corresponding regions in the full diagram. Each inset repeats all class curves and the perfect-calibration diagonal, so local deviations can be inspected without changing the underlying adaptive bins.

The aggregate ACE, top-label ECE, selected class's ACE, and per-bin details are saved to \texttt{results/calibration.json}. The experiment saves three vector PDF figures: the class-wise curve with zoom insets as \texttt{results/reliability\_diagram.pdf}, the fixed-width top-label ECE diagram as \texttt{results/ece\_reliability\_diagram.pdf}, and the selected class's adaptive ACE diagram as \texttt{results/classwise\_ace\_reliability\_diagram.pdf}. These vector graphics can be included directly in the thesis without rasterizing the plots.

The implementation measures ACE on the model's unadjusted softmax outputs; it does not modify model parameters or recalibrate probabilities. The bin-count rule and class-wise results are reported to make the estimate reproducible and interpretable. Temperature scaling from Equation~\eqref{eq:temperature-scaling} could be added as a separate post-hoc step, with its parameter fitted on a held-out calibration set rather than the test set.
